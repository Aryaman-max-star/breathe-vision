Swelling is the warning nobody measures.

A cell grows before it fails. Today that growth is judged by eye, if at all. BREATHE turns it into a number.

18.02.2027

From this date the EU Battery Regulation requires a digital battery passport for every EV battery placed on the EU market, kept up to date through its life.

15 to 35 °C

The window where lithium-ion cells perform best and last longest. Outside it, side reactions speed up.

2×

Calendar ageing roughly doubles for a pack kept at 35 °C instead of 25 °C, because the SEI layer grows faster with heat.

How a cell starts to swell.

Inside a sealed cell, a few side reactions slowly make solid films, metal growths and gas. Watch one cell go from healthy to swollen.

  1. 01

    A healthy cell

    Lithium ions shuttle through the separator between cathode and anode. The can is flat and the height is exactly as built.

  2. 02

    Stress: cold, fast or over charge

    Ions arrive faster than graphite can take them in. They deposit as metallic lithium on the anode surface: lithium plating.

  3. 03

    The SEI film thickens

    Electrolyte keeps reacting with the anode. The solid electrolyte interphase grows, consuming lithium and electrolyte.

  4. 04

    Gas is generated

    Electrolyte breaks down into gases. In a sealed can there is nowhere for them to go, so they collect at the top.

  5. 05

    Pressure becomes swelling

    Gas pressure and thicker electrodes push the walls out. The cell grows by fractions of a millimetre, long before anyone can see it.

  6. 06

    Heat closes the loop

    A swollen cell runs hotter, and heat speeds up every reaction above. This is the moment to catch it.

Two failures. Two different fixes.

Both modules can look fine. The pattern across the cells separates a bad cell from a bad cooling system, and they need opposite actions.

Case A / irregular chemical reaction

One cell grows on its own.

Plating or gas build-up inside a single cell. Its neighbours stay at their built height, so the outlier stands out against the median of the others.

SWELL / cell 3 / +0.60 mm
Isolate and replace cell 3. Test cells 2 and 4. Review its charge history for cold fast charging.

Case B / uneven heat distribution

The whole module warms unevenly.

A blocked cooling channel or a poor thermal pad heats one side. Every cell moves a little, together, and the temperature is up. No single cell is the problem.

A silhouette, read to a fraction of a pixel.

The camera looks along the top of the module against a white screen. Every column of pixels gives an edge, and hundreds of edges give each cell one precise height.

01

Scan

Slide the module in and press the knob. The camera averages 30 frames with focus and exposure locked.

02

Measure

Sub-pixel edge fitting on every column gives each cell a height, compared with its baseline and its neighbours.

03

Diagnose

Rules plus an anomaly model on the Arduino UNO Q separate swelling, heat, a badly seated cell and a bad image.

04

Act and record

A cell-specific action appears on the station, and every scan is stored per pack for its battery record.

At production scale

Built for the line.

The same measurement as the prototype, sized for a complete EV battery pack. An open pack goes into the fixture and is charged by the pack cycler, because swelling shows under charge, while a gantry scans every row of cells. The line gets a pass or hold in about two minutes. No cutting, no contact, no cloud needed to decide.

01

Fixture

Hardened locator rails, dowels and a pneumatic clamp put every pack in the same place, every time.

02

Backlit silhouette

A diffuse LED panel across the row turns each cell top into a crisp outline. Sub-pixel edge fitting does the rest.

03

Scanning gantry

A motorised gantry carries the camera and backlight row by row, so the whole pack is measured at the same resolution end to end.

04

Edge decision

The BREATHE edge unit, built on Arduino UNO Q, flags the cell on the spot and stores the scan against the pack ID.
